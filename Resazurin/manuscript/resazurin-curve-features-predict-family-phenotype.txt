ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Identifying stress-resilient oyster families is central to selective
breeding for summer survival, but the reference phenotype — survival
through a lethal heat or salinity challenge — destroys the animals it
measures and requires weeks of husbandry per cohort. Resazurin, a
redox-sensitive dye whose fluorescence tracks whole-animal metabolic rate,
offers a non-lethal, plate-reader-based alternative, yet most applications
reduce the resulting time course to a single number (slope or area under
the curve). Here we asked whether the full shape of the resazurin
fluorescence curve, summarised as ~20 quantitative features per
individual, could recover the survival ranking of nine USDA Pacific oyster
(Magallana gigas) families. We pooled seven independent lethal challenges
(33–36 °C, 35 °C + 9 ppt, and freshwater → 36 °C; n = 1,095 oysters) into a
composite, severity-normalised survival score per family, and separately
ran nine resazurin experiments (969 individual traces) under three
contexts: seawater heat (33–36 °C), freshwater at room temperature, and
freshwater combined with 36 °C heat. Curve features were extracted per
individual, averaged per family and context, screened by Spearman rank
correlation with the survival score, and validated by leave-one-family-out
cross-validation (LOFO-CV). Two coherent signals emerged. First, under
seawater heat, metabolic capacity traits (total and early AUC, maximum
slope, peak value, metabolic scope) were all negatively correlated with
survival (rho = −0.83 to −0.87, p < 0.01): families that ramped metabolism
most under heat survived least. Second, timing traits were positive
predictors: the time to steepest metabolic decline under freshwater + heat
was the single best predictor (rho = 0.90, p = 0.002; LOFO-CV rho = 0.81),
i.e., hardy families delayed their metabolic crash. A four-feature
resazurin index (time to steepest decline, curve stability, initial slope,
and depth of collapse, measured under freshwater + heat) predicted the
survival ranking out-of-sample (LOFO-CV rho = 0.79, RMSE = 7.9 score
units), correctly placing the hardiest (family 5) and most fragile (family
7) families at opposite ends. Signals were strongest when resazurin was
measured under a challenge that mirrored the survival stressor rather than
at rest. [PLOUGH: one to two sentences summarising the complementary
younger-seed resazurin and glycogen prediction results and their
agreement/disagreement with the adult-stage ranking.] These results
indicate that a few hours of non-lethal resazurin measurement under a
matched stressor can pre-screen candidate families for confirmatory
survival testing, and that curve shape — not just curve magnitude — carries
the predictive information.

1. INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summer mortality of Pacific oysters (Magallana gigas, formerly Crassostrea
gigas) is a persistent constraint on West Coast aquaculture, and family-
and line-level variation in survival is the raw material for selective
breeding programs such as the USDA ARS Pacific Shellfish Research Unit
program [ref: Plough/USDA program description; Molecular Breeding Program
refs] and the Molluscan Broodstock Program [ref]. The phenotype of
interest, however, is intrinsically expensive: survival under a
field-relevant stressor is measured by exposing tens of animals per family
to a lethal challenge and scoring mortality over days, or by planting
families in the field and waiting a season. Breeding programs therefore
have strong incentive to find rapid, non-lethal, laboratory-based proxies
that rank families in the same order as the lethal challenge.

Metabolic rate is an attractive candidate proxy. Whole-animal respirometry
has long linked metabolic phenotype to thermal tolerance in bivalves
[refs: Sokolova et al. 2012 energy-limited tolerance; Pörtner OCLTT;
bivalve metabolic depression refs], and the concept of metabolic depression
— a regulated down-shift of energy turnover under stress — is a recognised
survival strategy in intertidal molluscs [refs: Guppy & Withers 1999;
Storey & Storey]. But respirometry is low-throughput. Resazurin, a blue,
non-fluorescent redox dye that is reduced to pink, fluorescent resorufin
by cellular metabolic activity, has recently been adapted as a
high-throughput whole-organism proxy for metabolic rate in oysters
(Huffmyer et al. 2025, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.11.06.686367). Huffmyer et al.
showed that resazurin fluorescence correlates with oxygen consumption,
that it resolves temperature-dependent metabolic optima and tipping points,
and that families and lines differ in their resazurin response. That work
also reported a first indication that metabolic response to acute thermal
stress is related to predicted breeding performance.

Most resazurin analyses, including the initial protocol, summarise each
animal's fluorescence time course as a single scalar — typically the
fold-change at the final time point, the slope of a linear fit, or the
area under the curve (AUC). This discards information. A resazurin curve
measured during an acute challenge is a short time series that encodes
how quickly metabolism is stimulated, how high it goes, whether and when
it plateaus or declines, and how variable it is. If stress-resilient
families differ from fragile ones in the *dynamics* of their metabolic
response — for example, by depressing metabolism sooner or by avoiding an
early over-shoot — then features describing the shape of the curve should
predict survival better than magnitude alone.

Here we test that hypothesis directly. Within the SORMI (Summer Oyster
Resilience & Mortality Index) assay-development project we generated two
independent bodies of data on the same nine USDA M. gigas families: (i)
seven lethal survival challenges, pooled into a single severity-normalised
composite survival score per family, and (ii) nine resazurin experiments
spanning resting, freshwater, heat, and combined freshwater + heat
conditions. We extracted a comprehensive set of curve features from each
individual resazurin trace, aggregated them to the family level, and
asked: (1) which features, measured under which condition, best track the
family survival ranking; (2) whether a predictor selected in-sample
survives leave-one-family-out cross-validation; and (3) whether a simple
multi-feature index outperforms any single feature. Because the two data
sets were collected on different individuals, this is a family-level
(genetic/cohort) prediction rather than an individual-level one.

Experiments used juvenile (seed-stage) oysters in most cases; two
resazurin experiments (2026-04-15 and 2026-06-24) used adult animals

A labelling error affected two survival experiments (2026-05-11 and
2026-06-09) and several resazurin experiments: two groups were labelled
"9" and "9b" while family 2 was absent, and the correct assignment (one of
the two is family 2, the other family 9) was lost. Rather than guess, all
"9" and "9b" individuals were excluded from those two survival
experiments, and all "9b" individuals (n = 152 resazurin traces) were
excluded from the resazurin analysis. Family 9 is therefore scored from
the experiments in which it appears unambiguously, and results involving
family 9 are interpreted with this caveat.

2.2 Survival challenges and the composite survival phenotype

Seven lethal challenge experiments were run between 27 April and 7 July
2026 (Table 1). Stressors were acute heat in seawater (33 °C, 35 °C or
36 °C), heat combined with low salinity (35 °C, 9 ppt), and a freshwater
exposure followed by transfer to 36 °C. Container format varied (12- or
24-well plates with 4 mL seawater per well; ~100 mL cups; tank-room
"cans" and tri-pours), as did duration (42–188 h) and per-family sample
size (3–40 individuals). Mortality was scored at repeated time points by
gaping/lack of response [specify criterion], and each individual was
reduced to a (time of death or censoring, status) pair. Full protocols are
in the per-experiment README files in the project repository.

Because experiments differed in severity (overall mortality 33–99%), raw
survival is not comparable across dates. We therefore scored each family
within each experiment on two 0–1 metrics: the proportion alive at the
final time point (surv_prop) and the mean restricted lifetime divided by
the observation window (rel_surv_time; equal to the restricted mean
survival time normalised to the experiment duration, because every
individual was observed until death or the end of the experiment). Within
each experiment, each metric was converted to a percentile among the
families present in that experiment, 100 × (rank − 0.5)/n, removing the
experiment-severity effect. A family's composite survival score is the
mean of these percentiles across all experiments and both metrics
(range 0–100; higher = hardier). Secondary phenotypes, mean surv_prop and
mean rel_surv_time across experiments, were carried through as sensitivity
checks. Family differences within experiments were tested by log-rank
test. Analyses used R (v4.5.1) with the survival and ggsurvfit packages.

2.3 Resazurin metabolic assays

Resazurin assays followed the Roberts Lab protocol
(https://robertslab.github.io/resources/protocols/resazurin-assay/) as
described in Huffmyer et al. (2025). Briefly, a working solution was
prepared from a resazurin stock (final concentration [specify], with
0.1% DMSO and 1% penicillin/streptomycin/fungizone) in either artificial
seawater (Instant Ocean, [specify salinity]) or, for freshwater-stress
runs, dechlorinated tap water. Individual oysters were placed in 4 mL of
working solution in clear 12-well plates (juveniles) or in 70–100 mL in
individual cups (adults), with wells or cups lacking an oyster serving as
blanks. Plates or cups were held either at room temperature (~20–22 °C)
or in an incubator/water table at the target stress temperature (33 or
36 °C); solution temperature was logged with an infrared thermometer and
typically reached the target within 1–1.5 h. At each time point (every
0.5–1 h for 3–4 h, or hourly over longer runs; Table 2), fluorescence was
read directly on the 12-well plate or on a 200–250 µL aliquot transferred
to a 96-well plate, using a Synergy HTX plate reader (Agilent) with Gen5
software [specify excitation/emission wavelengths and gain]. Oysters were
photographed and their projected shell area (mm2) measured in ImageJ for
size normalisation.

Nine resazurin experiments were included (Table 2). Each was assigned to
one of three stress contexts on the basis of the medium and temperature:
seawater heat (sw_heat; 33–36 °C in seawater; 4 experiments), freshwater
at room temperature (freshwater_rt; 4 experiments), and freshwater
combined with 36 °C heat (fw_heat; 1 experiment, 2026-05-05). A pooled
"all" context combined every experiment. A tenth experiment (2026-07-13,
36 °C, adults from four families only) was not included in the
cross-experiment feature set. [verify; state reason]

2.4 Fluorescence processing

For each well and time point, raw relative fluorescence units (RFU) were
expressed as fold-change relative to that well's T0 reading. The mean
blank fold-change for the same plate and time point was subtracted to
give a blank-corrected fold-change (corrected_fc), which removes
temperature- and time-dependent autoreduction of the dye. A
size-normalised metric (corrected_fc divided by shell area, mm2;
metabolism_per_area) was computed in parallel. Wells flagged for
exclusion in the layout metadata (e.g., dead animals, pipetting errors)
and plates with inconsistent well counts across time points were dropped
before analysis. Both value metrics were carried through all downstream
steps.

2.5 Curve feature extraction

For every individual trace with at least two time points, we computed a
fixed panel of curve features from the (time, value) series (Table 3;
code in Resazurin/code/03.5-resazurin-curve-features-all-experiments.Rmd).
Features fell into four groups:

  Capacity / magnitude — final_value, peak_value, trough_value,
    auc_total (trapezoidal AUC), auc_early and auc_late (AUC before and
    after the median segment midpoint), delta_auc_late_minus_early,
    late_early_auc_ratio, metabolic_scope (peak − baseline), final_delta
    (final − baseline).
  Rate — initial_slope (first segment), vmax (maximum segment slope),
    min_slope (most negative segment slope).
  Timing — time_to_peak, time_to_trough, time_to_vmax,
    time_to_min_slope (time at which the steepest decline occurred),
    inflection_time (time of maximum change in slope).
  Depression / stability — depression_abs (peak − final, floored at 0),
    metabolic_depression_index (depression_abs / |metabolic_scope|),
    resilience_ratio (final / peak), stability_cv (coefficient of
    variation of the trace), and a categorical trajectory_class
    (monotonic_increase, rise_then_depress, depress_then_recover,
    monotonic_decrease, stable, complex) derived from the sign sequence
    of segment slopes.

baseline_value (the T0 reference, ≈1 by construction) was retained in the
feature table but excluded from all ranking and prediction analyses.
Within each experiment, the ability of each feature to discriminate
families was assessed by one-way ANOVA (feature ~ family) with eta-squared
as effect size and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across features, with
Kruskal–Wallis tests and Tukey HSD pairwise contrasts as checks.

2.6 Family-level feature matrix and screening

Per-individual features were averaged to family means within each
context × value-metric combination, and in the pooled "all" context. A
feature was retained only if family means were available for at least
six of the nine families. For every (context, metric, feature) we
computed Spearman rank correlation (primary; "does resazurin recover the
ranking?") and Pearson correlation (secondary) between the family-mean
feature and each survival phenotype across families (n = 8–9 families).
Because ~200 correlations were screened at n ≤ 9, unadjusted p-values are
reported for ranking purposes only, and we emphasise blocks of
mechanistically related features that move together over any single
value.

2.7 Cross-validated prediction

To estimate how well a predictor selected in-sample would place a new
family, we used leave-one-family-out cross-validation (LOFO-CV). For a
given feature, each family in turn was held out, a linear model
(composite score ~ family-mean feature) was fit to the remaining
families, and the held-out family's score was predicted. Performance was
summarised as the Spearman correlation between predicted and observed
scores across all held-out predictions (CV rho) and the root-mean-square
error (CV RMSE, in score units). Candidate features for LOFO-CV were the
three strongest |rho| features within each context × metric.

2.8 Multi-feature resazurin index

Within the context and value metric that produced the best
cross-validated single feature, we took the four features with the
largest |Spearman rho| with the composite score, z-scored each family
mean across families, multiplied by the sign of its correlation so that
higher always meant "more survival-like", and averaged the four signed
z-scores to give a resazurin index per family. Averaging pre-specified
z-scores rather than fitting a multiple regression keeps the number of
fitted parameters at two (intercept and slope of score ~ index) and
avoids over-fitting at n = 8. The index was then evaluated by the same
LOFO-CV procedure.

3. RESULTS

3.1 Families differ consistently in survival across stressors

The seven challenges pooled 1,095 oysters and ranged from mild (freshwater
→ 36 °C, 33% mortality; 33 °C on 27 April, 49%) to near-total (33 °C on
11 May, 99%; 35 °C on 6 July, 97%) (Table 1). Family differences within
experiments were significant by log-rank test in four of seven
experiments (p = 0.0008 to 0.024) and marginal in two others (p = 0.05–
0.06). After within-experiment percentile scoring, the composite survival
score ranged from 71.0 (family 5) to 35.3 (family 7) (Table 4). Family 5
was hardiest, followed by families 9 (63.2) and 2 (59.0); families 8, 3
and 1 were intermediate (52.7, 48.8, 46.9); and families 6, 10 and 7 were
least hardy (40.8, 36.7, 35.3). The ranking was broadly consistent across
individual experiments [insert family_consistency figure reference and a
sentence on the range of per-experiment percentiles for the top and
bottom families]. Composite score, mean surv_prop and mean rel_surv_time
were positively correlated with each other [insert rho].

3.2 Resazurin curve features discriminate families within experiments

Nine resazurin experiments yielded 969 individual fluorescence traces
(99–162 per experiment; 4–14 time points each; Table 2). Family explained
a substantial share of variance in curve features within experiments. In
the juvenile 12-well-plate runs, trough_value was the single most
family-discriminating feature (eta-squared 0.82–0.95 in the freshwater
and freshwater + heat experiments; BH-adjusted p < 10^-28) [note: check
whether trough_value's extreme F-values in the freshwater runs reflect
near-degenerate values, e.g., troughs pinned at the T0 reference, and
consider whether it should be interpreted as a shape trait]. In the
seawater-heat experiments the best discriminators were late-phase
capacity traits (delta_auc_late_minus_early, peak_value, metabolic_scope,
final_value; eta-squared ≈ 0.20, BH p = 0.04 on 2026-06-02) and timing
traits (time_to_min_slope, inflection_time). Trajectory classes also
differed by family: under freshwater + heat, every individual of family
5, 6 and 8 showed a monotonic increase in corrected fold-change over 4 h,
whereas 64% of family 7 and 45% of family 3 individuals rose and then
depressed (Table 5).

3.3 Screening: capacity under heat is negatively, and timing positively,
    associated with survival

Across 8 context × metric combinations, [insert number] features passed
the ≥6-family filter and were screened against the composite score
(Figure 1; Table 6; full table in feature_phenotype_correlations.csv).
Two coherent blocks of features emerged.

Capacity block (negative). Under seawater heat (sw_heat, size-normalised
metric), every metabolic-capacity feature was negatively correlated with
survival: auc_total (rho = −0.87, p = 0.005), vmax (−0.87, p = 0.005),
auc_early (−0.85, p = 0.006), auc_late, final_value, metabolic_scope and
peak_value (all −0.83, p = 0.008), and final_delta (−0.77, p = 0.02)
(n = 9 families). The same features were negatively correlated using the
non-normalised metric (auc_total and vmax rho = −0.73, p = 0.03) and in
the pooled "all" context (vmax rho = −0.78; depression_abs rho = −0.80,
p = 0.01). Families that ramped their metabolic signal highest and fastest
when heated in seawater were the families that survived least in the
lethal challenges. The capacity block was also the strongest predictor of
the secondary phenotype mean surv_prop (auc_early rho = −0.92, p = 0.001;
auc_total and vmax rho = −0.90, p = 0.002).

Timing / stability block (positive). The strongest single association in
the entire screen was time_to_min_slope measured under freshwater + heat
(fw_heat): rho = 0.90 (p = 0.002; Pearson r = 0.87, p = 0.005; n = 8
families, family 2 not present in that experiment) for both value
metrics (which coincide for a timing feature). The same feature measured
under freshwater at room temperature was also strongly positive (rho =
0.83, p = 0.015), and time_to_peak under seawater heat was positive (rho
= 0.73, p = 0.03). Hardy families reached their steepest metabolic
decline later, i.e., they sustained their trajectory longer before
crashing. Under fw_heat, stability_cv was negatively associated with
survival (rho = −0.83, p = 0.015; hardy families had steadier traces),
initial_slope was positively associated (rho = 0.76, p = 0.04;
size-normalised) and depression_abs negatively (rho = −0.66). The pooled
trough_value (size-normalised) was positively associated with survival
(rho = 0.82, p = 0.01; Pearson r = 0.87, p = 0.002).

Context mattered. The strongest associations came from runs that imposed
a stressor (sw_heat or fw_heat); the freshwater-at-room-temperature
context produced weaker and less consistent correlations, and no resting
seawater run was available for comparison [note: state explicitly that
no seawater/room-temperature control run of these families exists in the
feature set].

3.4 Cross-validation: timing under freshwater + heat generalises best

Twenty-four candidate single features (top three per context × metric)
were evaluated by LOFO-CV (Table 7). time_to_min_slope under fw_heat had
the highest out-of-sample rank agreement (CV rho = 0.81, CV RMSE = 7.7
score units; in-sample rho = 0.90), followed by pooled trough_value (CV
rho = 0.75, RMSE = 7.0) and pooled depression_abs (CV rho = 0.72, RMSE =
9.1). The seawater-heat capacity features, despite in-sample rho of
−0.87, generalised less well (vmax CV rho = 0.67, RMSE = 14.0; auc_total
CV rho = 0.65, RMSE = 13.3), reflecting their more skewed family-mean
distributions (Pearson r ≈ −0.64 vs Spearman −0.87) and the leverage of
individual families in a linear fit at n = 9. stability_cv under fw_heat
fell from in-sample rho −0.83 to CV rho 0.52. Figure 2 shows observed
versus family-mean time_to_min_slope; family 5 (score 71) had the latest
steepest decline and family 7 (score 35) among the earliest.

3.5 A four-feature resazurin index predicts the survival ranking
    out-of-sample

Within the fw_heat / corrected_fc combination, the four features with
the largest |rho| were time_to_min_slope (+0.90), stability_cv (−0.83),
initial_slope (+0.71) and depression_abs (−0.66). Their sign-aligned,
z-scored average — the resazurin index — was correlated with the
composite score at rho = 0.81 in-sample and rho = 0.79 under LOFO-CV,
with CV RMSE 7.9 score units (Figure 3; Table 8). The index ranged from
−1.44 (family 7, observed score 35.3, CV-predicted 32.1) to +1.27 (family
5, observed 71.0, predicted 64.6), with family 9 second on both scales
(index 1.09; observed 63.2, predicted 62.5). The intermediate families
(8, 3, 1, 10) were predicted within 4–8 score units of observation; the
largest miss was family 6 (index 0.31, predicted 53.2 vs observed 40.8).
In words, a "survivor-like" resazurin profile under freshwater + heat is
one that rises promptly, stays steady, does not collapse far from its
peak, and reaches its steepest decline late (Figure 4, heatmap of the ten
top features by survival rank).

4. DISCUSSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Curve shape, not magnitude, carries the predictive signal

The central result is that the survival ranking of nine oyster families
across seven lethal challenges could be recovered from a few hours of
non-lethal resazurin measurement — but only when the fluorescence time
course was decomposed into shape features and measured under a matching
stressor. The best single predictor was not the AUC or final fold-change
that conventional analyses report, but the time at which the trace
reached its steepest decline (time_to_min_slope). A four-feature index
combining that timing feature with trace stability, initial slope and
depth of collapse recovered the ranking out-of-sample (LOFO-CV rho =
0.79), placing the hardiest and most fragile families correctly at the
extremes. This supports the premise motivating the study: the dynamics
of the metabolic response to acute stress are phenotypically informative
in a way that a single summary scalar is not.

4.2 Hardy families restrain and delay their metabolic response

The direction of the capacity signal is the counter-intuitive finding.
Under seawater heat, every measure of metabolic output — AUC, maximum
slope, peak, scope — was negatively correlated with survival. Families
that ramped up resazurin reduction most when heated were the families
that died fastest in the lethal challenges. This is a coherent block of
six to eight mutually correlated features moving together, which is what
makes it credible at n = 9 families. Huffmyer et al. (2025) reported an
analogous pattern in their acute thermal stress experiments [verify
wording: oysters exhibiting greater metabolic stimulation under acute
heat showed poorer outcomes / lower predicted performance], and the
result aligns with the long-standing view that regulated metabolic
depression, rather than sustained metabolic stimulation, is the adaptive
response of intertidal bivalves to acute thermal stress [refs: Sokolova
2013; Guppy & Withers 1999; Pörtner]. Metabolic over-shoot under heat may
reflect a failure to suppress ATP demand, accelerated depletion of
reserves and accumulation of oxidative damage; the timing block —
hardy families delay their steepest decline and show steadier traces —
is consistent with families that sustain a controlled response longer
before the system fails. We note that the resazurin signal is a
whole-animal reduction rate that integrates gill ventilation, tissue
perfusion and cellular redox state, so "metabolic capacity" here should
not be equated narrowly with oxygen consumption [ref Huffmyer
validation].

4.3 Measure under the stressor you want to predict

Associations were strongest in the two contexts that imposed a stressor
(seawater heat; freshwater + heat) and weakest under freshwater at room
temperature. The best single feature and the index both came from the
single freshwater + 36 °C experiment — the resazurin condition most
similar to the most discriminating survival challenge (freshwater → 36
°C, log-rank p < 0.0001). For a breeding program this is a practical
recommendation: a resazurin screen should be run under an acute version
of the field stressor of interest, not at rest. It also raises the
possibility that a battery of short challenges (heat, low salinity,
combined) could yield stressor-specific family rankings.

4.4 A screen, not an oracle

Several limitations bound the interpretation. First, n = 9 families
(eight for the fw_heat context) means that any single correlation is
fragile, ~200 feature × context × metric combinations were screened, and
unadjusted p-values are reported for ranking rather than inference. Our
mitigations — emphasising coherent feature blocks, LOFO-CV, and a
pre-specified z-score index rather than a fitted multiple regression —
reduce but do not eliminate optimism in the reported CV rho, because
feature selection itself was performed on the full data set. A fully
nested CV or, better, validation on new families is required. Second,
the resazurin and survival cohorts were different individuals of the same
families, so this is a family-level prediction; individual-level
prediction (resazurin then survival on the same animals) has not been
tested. Third, the 9/9b labelling ambiguity removed family 2 from several
experiments and leaves family 9's score based on five experiments.
Fourth, the survival challenges varied in format and severity; the
percentile-based composite is robust to this by design but averages over
potentially stressor-specific rankings. Fifth, the fw_heat context is a
single experiment, so its leading role could partly reflect a
favourable day; replication of that condition is the highest priority.
Finally, the very high F-statistics for trough_value in the freshwater
runs suggest near-degenerate values in some families and that feature
should be treated cautiously as a shape trait [verify].

4.6 Toward a composite resilience index

The SORMI project is assembling several rapid assays (resazurin,
glycogen, Na+/K+-ATPase, citrate synthase, lactate dehydrogenase, total
antioxidant capacity) with the aim of a composite Summer Oyster
Resilience & Mortality Index. The present results show that resazurin
curve features alone already recover most of the family survival ranking
and suggest how additional assays should be integrated: as sign-aligned,
z-scored family-level features under a matched acute stressor, evaluated
by leave-one-family-out prediction, with preference for features that
generalise rather than those with the highest in-sample correlation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Decomposing resazurin fluorescence time courses into shape features
revealed two robust, mechanistically coherent signals of family-level
heat survival in Pacific oysters: hardy families restrain their metabolic
stimulation under heat and delay their metabolic decline, whereas fragile
families over-shoot early and collapse. A four-feature index measured
during a 4-h freshwater + heat exposure predicted the family survival
ranking out-of-sample (LOFO-CV rho ≈ 0.8). [PLOUGH: one sentence on
whether seed-stage resazurin and/or glycogen reproduced this ranking.]
Resazurin curve-feature profiling is therefore a candidate non-lethal
pre-screen for family selection, to be confirmed with additional families
and prospective validation.
